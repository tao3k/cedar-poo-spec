#+TITLE: RFC 0002: Cedar Policy Module Composition
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil

* Contract

=PolicyModules.Model.compile= turns a C4 hierarchy of policy modules into
an ordinary Cedar =Policies= value. A module has a name, ordered parents,
and explicit edits. The edits are =extend=, =overlay=, and =remove=.

Compilation linearizes the requested root with Lean-POO C4, applies the
base modules first, and applies descendant edits last. =extend= introduces
a new policy ID. =overlay= replaces an inherited policy with the same ID.
=remove= retires an inherited policy ID. An ID may be reintroduced later by
an explicit =extend=. The compiler preserves a deterministic policy order.

Compilation returns a typed error for an inconsistent C4 graph, a duplicate
ID on extend, an absent ID on overlay or remove, or competing sibling edits
to the same ID. A descendant may change an ancestor's policy. A sibling may
not silently replace another sibling's edit, even when C4 gives a linear
order; the conflicting parent modules must be reconciled before compilation.

The output is passed to Cedar's own validator and authorizer. This feature
does not reinterpret Cedar's permit/forbid rules, policy errors, or schema.
Its purpose is to make policy provenance and evolution explicit before
evaluation and to connect resulting policies to Lean-POO proof objects.
=compileWithTrace= returns every successful edit and each surviving policy
with its introducing and latest editing module. =compileWithProvenance=
projects those surviving policies; =compile= projects the ordinary Cedar
policy set from the same result.
=compileRevision= pairs two traced compilations. =changedPolicyIds= lists
added, removed, or body-changed policy IDs, regardless of edit provenance.
The separate =Revision.authorizationPatch= maps a revision to the Cedar
authorization proof object. It emits an empty patch only when the complete
ordered policy lists are equal; otherwise it replaces the policy-set key.
Consequently a no-edit descendant can reuse the whole certificate, while a
changed policy set selects Cedar's whole-set validation obligation.
=Revision.freshPolicies= selects policy bodies absent from the baseline;
=Revision.validateAfter= derives Cedar's whole-set =validate= premise from
baseline validation and fresh-body Lean-POO certificates. Each
=PolicyValidation.Snapshot= has one obligation depending on its policy body
and Cedar schema. =PolicyValidation.Bundle= contains independent certificates
for the current policy list; =Bundle.reviseSameSchema= carries certificates
for identical bodies and requires certificates only for fresh bodies. No
historical obligation remains attached to a removed body. The derivation
uses the upstream validator's per-policy =forM= structure, so removed and
reordered bodies need no new typechecking proof when the schema is unchanged.
The authorization proof object's dependency key remains the complete ordered
policy set; its Cedar premise is reclosed from the policy bundle.
=Revision.authorizationPatch_object= proves that applying the diagnostic patch
to the baseline produces the exact revised proof object.
=Revision.authorizationCertificate= reuses the baseline schema, request, and
entity premises and derives the revised policy premise from fresh-body checks;
callers supply only the baseline certificate and those fresh checks.
=Revision.patchedAuthorizationCertificate= exposes the same result on the
Lean-POO patched proof object.
If the schema changes, every per-policy obligation and all four
schema-dependent authorization obligations are invalidated.
=Revision.authorizationCertificateWithSchema= accepts new per-policy
certificates, schema well-formedness, request validation, and entity
validation. It does not reuse old-schema policy certificates; finer schema
dependency slicing would need an additional Cedar-backed theorem.
=Revision.authorizationPatchWithSchema_object= proves that a combined policy
and schema patch materializes exactly the revised proof object.
=Revision.patchedAuthorizationCertificateWithSchema= closes that patched
object from the new-schema evidence.
For same-schema execution, =Revision.incrementalValidate= requires a proof
that the baseline policy set passed Cedar validation. It delegates to the
validated-set cache and is proven equivalent to Cedar's complete result.
=Revision.tryRefresh= also requires proof that the cache's stored policy list
equals this revision's baseline before it attempts to advance the cache.
The governance example checks a reorder explicitly: the fresh-body set is
empty, yet the ordered policy list changes and therefore the authorization
patch still touches the policy key.

* Executable scenario

[[file:../../Examples/Scenarios/README.org][The tenant document migration]] demonstrates a broad legacy permit that
survives a strict overlay. An added forbid blocks untrusted devices; removal
of the legacy permit then blocks cross-tenant reads. The executable Lean
scenario checks every revision against the Cedar evaluator, validates every
compiled policy set, verifies the final requests have no runtime policy
errors, and closes a Cedar authorization soundness certificate.

[[file:../../Examples/Health/README.org][The clinical break-glass case]] composes independent privacy,
device-security, and emergency-access branches over a shared base. It
checks a twelve-request decision matrix, a risky pre-integration branch,
policy provenance, and a final Cedar soundness certificate. Session grants
and audit remain responsibilities of the calling application.

[[file:../../Examples/Governance/README.org][The attested governance case]] isolates a platform attestation overlay
from independent data-owner and compliance branches. It checks the changed
policy ID, graph impact, one pending policy-validation obligation, and a
no-edit descendant whose revision has no pending obligations. Its updated
certificate closes the policy-validation obligation by checking only the
new attestation policy body.

This scenario shows fewer repeated policy records for four revisions, but
does not claim a measured LOC or maintenance-cost reduction. Broader claims
require additional scenario corpora and change-cost measurements.

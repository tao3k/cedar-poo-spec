* Research case and exact boundary

[[https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00181v2][CHAI (Burbano et al., 2026)]]
demonstrates environmental indirect prompt injection against an LVLM-based
driving command layer. In its crosswalk experiment, the benign high-level
decision was to brake while pedestrians crossed; an attacker-controlled
visual sign changed the model's decision toward acceleration. The paper
reports attack success on its evaluated DriveLM images and a small robotic
vehicle. It does not report a production fleet compromise.

[[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.29114][ReasonBreak (Teymoorianfard et al.,
2026)]] independently studies reasoning-enabled driving VLA models and
finds that realistic text perturbations can change reasoning and
trajectories in closed-loop simulation. Its trajectory attacks can bypass
an interface that sees only high-level commands. [[https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24935][SABER (Wu et al.,
2026)]] automates iterative black-box instruction attacks on robotic VLA
models in the LIBERO manipulation benchmark. SABER supports the concern
that attackers can automate search; its reported results are *not*
automotive measurements.

| Source observation | Proposed Cedar/POO projection | What remains unverified |
|--------------------+-------------------------------+-------------------------|
| CHAI promotes attacker-controlled visual text into an unsafe driving command. | A high-level =accelerate= proposal enters an authorization boundary; a separate provenance owner can veto promotion of scene text into instruction authority. | The example does not detect the prompt injection or establish the provenance flag. |
| CHAI's occupied crosswalk should produce braking, but the attack elicits acceleration. | A separate safety owner overlays acceleration permission with an independent =pedestriansPresent= veto; braking remains available. | The example does not authenticate sensors or prove safe actuation. |
| ReasonBreak also manipulates trajectories and reports timing effects. | The command boundary has an explicit scope; its Cedar policy cannot certify trajectories or real-time control latency. | Low-level VLA outputs and control-loop timing need a different verified runtime layer. |
| Automated and transferable attacks can recur as models change. | An incident owner can quarantine acceleration for an affected cohort, then remove that local veto after externally established recovery. | The paper does not prescribe this fleet response; it is our proposed policy evolution. |

All identities, cohorts, route decisions, provenance flags, and independent
perception facts in [[file:CommandBoundary.lean][CommandBoundary.lean]] are
illustrative. The host must bind them to the same command proposal and
observation, authenticate the planner and independent sensor, enforce the
decision before the relevant action, and choose a separate safe fallback.
The Lean proof is conditional on those inputs. This case does not claim that
Cedar or Lean-POO alone prevents CHAI, certifies a VLA, or meets an
automotive control-cycle deadline.

* Decision matrix

=A= means Cedar allows the named request; =D= means it denies. Unless a
column says otherwise, route approval is present, the crosswalk is clear,
scene text has not been promoted, and the model is not marked affected.
The matrix is derived from the named Lean cases and replayed through the
official Rust Cedar authorizer.

| POO root | Occupied crosswalk accelerate | Clear route accelerate | Scene-text-promoted accelerate | Affected cohort accelerate | Brake at crosswalk |
|----------+------------------------------+------------------------+-------------------------------+-----------------------------+--------------------|
| =Base= | A | A | A | A | A |
| =Safety= | D | A | A | A | A |
| =Provenance= | A | A | D | A | A |
| =Integrated= | D | A | D | A | A |
| =Incident= | D | A | D | D | A |
| =Recovered= | D | A | D | A | A |

The =Safety= and =Provenance= rows each leave a different hole. Their C4
composition closes both for the modeled inputs. =Incident= affects only
the marked cohort and leaves braking and hazard reporting available.
=Recovered= removes only the cohort veto; the pedestrian and provenance
rules remain. A false =sceneTextPromoted= value can miss an injected sign;
the independent pedestrian veto still handles the specific crosswalk case
when its sensor fact is correct.

Six standalone Cedar revisions contain 23 materialized policy occurrences.
Lean declares six distinct policy bodies and seven POO edits, including one
overlay and one removal; the generated Cedar files remain standalone.
This count describes source maintenance, not a measured speedup. Lean proves
that the incident adds exactly one fresh policy and recovery adds none.
The unchanged policy certificates are reused when the authorization
certificate is reclosed, and Cedar's upstream soundness theorem applies
to the resulting snapshots. The [[file:CommandBoundaryExport.lean][exporter]]
materializes the same named requests for Rust Cedar conformance.

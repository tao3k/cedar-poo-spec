* Scope and sources

[[https://www.iso.org/standard/70918.html][ISO/SAE 21434]] defines lifecycle
cybersecurity risk management for road-vehicle E/E systems; it does not
prescribe one technology or policy language. [[https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/5/oj/eng/pdf][UN R155 Annex 5]]
provides a baseline of threats and corresponding mitigations to consider
in vehicle risk assessment. [[https://openstd.samr.gov.cn/bzgk/std/newGbInfo?hcno=2DB552CAA58F589705C3DC7AD47AC2AB][GB 44495-2024]] is the current
Chinese vehicle-cybersecurity standard. [[https://unece.org/transport/documents/2021/03/standards/un-regulation-no-156-software-update-and-software-update][UN R156]]
addresses software-update management, a related lifecycle boundary.

The mission failure modes below come from the
[[https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06996][mission-level runtime assurance study]];
[[https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29114][ReasonBreak]] motivates testing an
untrusted VLA producer. The concrete risk rows, owner allocation, and
policy treatments are *this example's projection*, not official ratings
or controls prescribed by those sources. A real TARA still needs
item-specific damage scenarios, attack paths, impact and feasibility
ratings, risk decisions, and accountable approval.

[[https://www.usenix.org/conference/vehiclesec26/presentation/woo][Woo and Lee's Physical AI TARA study]] combines automotive TARA with
AI and physical-layer attack taxonomies. [[https://www.iso.org/standard/83303.html][ISO/PAS 8800:2024]] addresses
vehicle-level safety risks from AI output insufficiencies and errors.
These are complementary research and safety inputs; neither makes a
Cedar permit decision an impact rating or a safety assurance claim.

* AI and hardware entry points

The current mission case exercises one VLA-style planner boundary.
Vehicle TARA must also consider other entry points before claiming
coverage. The following sources establish distinct interfaces and
failure mechanisms; they are not all implementations in this repo.

| Entry point | Source-backed problem | Authorization or evidence boundary | Example status |
|-------------+-----------------------+------------------------------------+----------------|
| VLA planner | Mission-level unsafe successor despite a locally valid command | Bind command to independently checked successor and later observation | Executable Mission case |
| Cabin speech and language model | [[https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19630][Afroze et al.]] report false executes in a vehicle voice-command benchmark; [[https://www.usenix.org/conference/raid2020/presentation/wang-shu][SIEVE]] studies adversarial in-vehicle speech sources | Speaker provenance, role, authentication, vehicle state, available tool, and pre-action decision | Source-backed research; no voice adapter yet |
| Wearable-to-vehicle automation | [[https://zhiqlin.github.io/file/SP24.pdf][Zhang et al.]] study smart-glasses-triggered vehicle control chains without user verification | Authenticate each cross-device command and bind it to the intended vehicle and action | Source-backed research; no wearable adapter yet |
| Embedded sensing and CAN AI | [[https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc24/presentation/xu-he][Xu et al.]] implement CAN intrusion detection on embedded hardware; [[https://www.usenix.org/conference/vehiclesec26/presentation/ali][Ali and Malik]] study counterfeit or compromised embedded-device identity | Preserve sensor/device provenance, model revision, timing, and authority across ECU/controller boundaries | Source-backed research; no hardware adapter yet |

The wearable link is a cross-device Vehicle/TARA input. A medical
wearable's own clinical risks belong in its separate health case. A
shared POO treatment can be reused across these entry points only
after its concrete Cedar request schema and authority assumptions are
checked for each interface.

The =clear= row is a positive control, not a threat. These six roots are

[[file:TaraProjection.lean][TaraProjection.lean]] ties these axes to
the verified 42-cell matrix. The underlying POO model declares six
owner edits. Six standalone Cedar revisions materialize 18 policy
occurrences; those runtime artifacts are *not* compressed away. The
compression is in the source of change: in the controlled fallback
maintenance experiment, one =Base= edit propagates through all six
roots, whereas six independently maintained Cedar snapshots require
six condition edits. Official Rust Cedar matches Lean on the 68
original decisions and 12 changed-case decisions. The
[[file:../Mission/README.org][mission case]] gives the exact scope and
limitations of that comparison.

* TARA handoff to executable treatment

TARA remains the owner of asset and damage analysis, attack feasibility,
impact, risk acceptance, and approval. Its output can identify a
treatment objective and an accountable owner. Cedar then enforces a
specific authorization decision; Lean checks the encoded decision and
its assumptions; Lean-POO composes the treatment changes and tracks
affected proof obligations. Runtime reference verification, sensor
provenance, and actuator effects remain host responsibilities. This is
an integration boundary, not a claim that TARA is defective.

Modular TARA methods already exist: the
[[https://saemobilus.sae.org/articles/mosaic-tara-a-comprehensive-tara-methodology-automotive-cybersecurity-2026-26-0609][MOSAIC-TARA study]] explicitly addresses assessment reuse across ECUs
and vehicle platforms. The comparison here is with a standalone TARA
matrix and independently maintained Cedar snapshots, not a claim that
all TARA methods lack reuse.

* Minimal review handoff

The 42 cells count *policy decisions* across seven axes and six
composition roots. They are not 42 completed risk assessments. A common
POO treatment owner can produce the same Cedar rule for several vehicle
contexts while their impact and attack-feasibility judgments differ.
MOSAIC-TARA explicitly notes that the same component may have different
damage impact depending on context. Therefore the adapter does not
decide whether a risk rating is reusable.

[[file:TaraHandoff.lean][TaraHandoff.lean]] accepts only a source ID,
work-product ID, revision, approval reference, treatment-goal ID, and
the linked executable treatment publication. It does not parse the
asset catalog, attack tree, impact model, risk matrix, or residual-risk
calculations. A host-supplied verifier checks the external approval.
An unverified reference produces =taraOwnerReview=. A changed TARA
reference, goal, or Cedar publication produces =treatmentOwnerReview= so
the executable link and affected certificates can be checked. Both
obligations may be emitted together. The example verifier is an
explicit assumption, not a TARA authority implementation.

| Event | Source owner | Treatment owner |
|-------+--------------+-----------------|
| Verified reference and unchanged publication | Retains TARA authority | No new link review emitted |
| TARA reference or goal changes, with valid approval | Supplies reviewed work product | Recheck goal-to-policy mapping and affected certificates |
| TARA reference fails external verification | Re-establish approval and source identity | Hold and recheck the link |
| Cedar publication changes | Retains TARA authority | Recheck the composed treatment and publication |

This gate only routes work. It cannot prove that an external approval is
authentic, that an unchanged assessment is still current, or that a
policy is suitable for a new context. The host must implement approval
verification and bind the reference revision to the actual source
work product.

The [[https://github.com/ASRG/openXSAM][ASRG openXSAM repository]] provides
a public XML exchange model for automotive risk information, including
risk and control schemas. It is a plausible upstream interchange format,
but this example neither vendors its GPL-licensed schema nor implements
an XML importer. [[https://github.com/leonkalema/QuickTARA][QuickTARA]] reports
JSON among its export formats; its public repository does not establish
a universal industry output schema. Neither GitHub repository is treated
as the authority for a real vehicle assessment. The TARA owner supplies
the small, approved projection above, regardless of its authoring tool.

* Relationship to POO Flow and Lambda Episteme

The candidate path can use the existing POO Flow source-admission and
Lambda Episteme Case/Scenario machinery without moving TARA semantics
into Lean-POO. Lambda Episteme can declare the selected external
reference and evidence source; POO Flow can admit a composed candidate
and return a typed receipt. Admission is not authorization to execute.
The Cedar adapter then maps an approved treatment goal to a specific
Lean-POO policy composition, and the application host verifies the
external approval before publishing or acting. This cross-repository
path is a proposed integration; =TaraHandoff.lean= currently checks only
the local reference-to-publication change boundary.

|------------+---------------+--------------|
| Modular risk assessment | MOSAIC-TARA proposes assessment reuse with context-sensitive damage classification | Consumes a TARA reference; does not duplicate ratings |
| Physical AI threat discovery | Woo and Lee combine automotive, AI, and physical-layer taxonomies | Models one untrusted-planner path; no attack-library completeness claim |

| Vehicle safety and regulatory approval | Requires a vehicle-specific assurance process | Not established by this example |

For VLA-driven vehicles, the treatment handoff needs model and prompt
provenance, command and horizon binding, reference and observation
authority, model-update revision, and evidence that a physical fallback
was actually applied. The current example implements only a small part
of that handoff: ID and tick binding, a source-backed offline trajectory
comparison, hypothetical post-decision observations, and policy-level
fallback authorization.

* Decision and observation states

This diagram shows the intended integration boundary. The host owns
reference verification, sensor provenance, and physical handoff; the
example exposes the two Lean checks and Cedar authorization.

#+begin_src mermaid
stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> Proposed
    Proposed --> Denied: Cedar denies command
    Proposed --> ReferenceCheck: Cedar allows command
    ReferenceCheck --> FallbackRequested: reference invalid or path outside envelope
    ReferenceCheck --> Executing: independent reference accepted
    Executing --> Executing: verified observation within envelope
    Executing --> FallbackRequested: observation invalid, late, or outside envelope
    FallbackRequested --> HostHandoff: Cedar authorizes fallback request
    HostHandoff --> [*]: host applies physical controller
    Denied --> [*]
#+end_src

The diagram is an interface contract, not a demonstrated physical
controller. In particular, comparing execution only with the attacked
VLA's own prediction can miss a self-consistent attack; the
[[file:../Mission/TrajectoryReplay.lean][Lean replay]] includes that
counterexample.

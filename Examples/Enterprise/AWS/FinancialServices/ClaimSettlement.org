* Evidence and composition boundary

The [[https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/industries/agentic-ai-in-financial-services-choosing-the-right-pattern-for-multi-agent-systems/][AWS financial-services workflow article]] describes autonomous claims
adjudication with separate notice, appraisal, and settlement phases. The
[[https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/industries/zero-touch-claims-how-pc-insurers-can-optimize-claims-processing-using-aws-ai-ml-services/][AWS claims-settlement discussion]] says settlement can require sending
payments to customers or repairers and updating the claims system of record.
That is the business reason for linking claim data to a payment owner. The
[[https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/secure-ai-agents-with-policy-and-lambda-interceptors-in-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-gateway/][AWS lakehouse example]] supplies actual claim-data Gateway tools and role/
geography Cedar restrictions. The [[https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/build-a-multi-account-ai-agent-with-agentcore-gateway-and-mcp/][AWS multi-account banking example]] supplies
actual Retail and Transaction MCP tools plus a four-call transfer route.
[[https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/architecture/modernizing-kyc-with-aws-serverless-solutions-and-agentic-ai-for-financial-services/][AWS's agentic KYC architecture]] also shows why an enterprise authorization
workflow crosses document analysis, risk and compliance, transaction
monitoring, and core banking systems rather than ending at one Gateway.

[[file:ClaimSettlement.lean][ClaimSettlement.lean]] composes the two published *examples* into a proposed
insurance-to-bank settlement authorization plan. AWS does not claim these
two samples are one deployed product. The composite does not invent an AWS
settlement API: its banking segment uses the published =get_balance=,
=transfer_funds=, and two =update_balance= calls. The insurance segment
uses the published =query_claims= and =get_claim_details= calls.

* Where POO provides leverage

Both Cedar models import [[file:../AgentCore/Gateway.lean][Gateway.lean]] for AgentCore entity types, schema
entry, and scoped policy construction. The insurance model C4-composes
Role, EU, and Restricted owner branches, then extends them with a
=FailClosed= veto.
The banking model C4-composes Retail, Transaction, Lending, and Protocol
owner branches. Its [[file:MultiAccountBanking/Lineage.lean][lineage]] dispatches over credential mode and LOB
owner to reuse shared identity requirements. The composite imports those
modules; it does not copy their policies or credential rules.

An incident branch =TransferPaused= overlays only the Transaction owner's
permit to remove =transfer_funds=. The rest of that owner's tools remain
available; =TransferResumed= overlays the original permit back. The bank
revision reports exactly one changed policy ID. In the cross-domain plan,
the pause blocks settlement while the claims decisions remain the same.
[[file:ClaimSettlementRevision.lean][ClaimSettlementRevision.lean]] uses the public LeanPOO =ProofObject=,
=Patch.set=, =append=, and =reuse= APIs: the bank-key patch invalidates the
bank obligation, while the insurance obligation's certificate is reused
because its declared dependency is untouched. This is a concrete proof
reuse boundary over the two Gateway decisions, not a claim that an
authorization result survives a change to its own policy.

The Lean result is a *Gateway authorization plan*. For a US adjuster and
banking operator, both insurance calls and the composed banking route are
allowed. Either Retail or Transaction alone breaks the route. EU geography
or absent/unknown geography under =FailClosed= stops the insurance segment,
even when banking locally permits its calls. The source-shaped insurance
root still allows absent geography; this is why the extension matters to a
cross-domain route rather than just a single policy decision.

* Threats and missing evidence

| Threat or failure | Observable result in this model | Required external authority |
|-------------------+---------------------------------+-----------------------------|
| Geography missing or =UNKNOWN= | =FailClosed= denies the claims calls | Trusted interceptor value and deployment evidence |
| EU claim-data request | Insurance owner veto denies the plan | Geography mapping and Lake Formation data access |
| Only one bank LOB owner installed | The four-call bank route fails | Published owner policy set at the Gateway |
| Transaction transfer paused by an incident overlay | Settlement route fails while claims and bank read calls remain available | Transaction owner revision and fresh bank evidence |
| Policyholder reads a claim then initiates an unrelated payment | Gateway decisions can still allow the plan | Claim ownership, case binding, trusted actor handoff |
| Agent omits debit or credit after transfer | Per-call Gateway authorization does not detect completion | Bank workflow atomicity, idempotency, compensation |
| Forged or stale cross-organization delegation | The Gateway plan cannot establish a trusted handoff | Independent identity and case-bound delegation receipt |

The =policyholderToolAccessIsNotClaimProvenance= Lean result deliberately
exposes an insufficiency: Cedar tool permission is not proof that the
returned claim belongs to the bank payment case. The plan keeps adjuster
and bank operator as distinct principals. Their handoff is an external
requirement. None of the Lean theorems claims AWS runtime
execution, data lineage, or settlement completion.

* Research case and exact boundary

[[https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15888][Bounded Agents (2026)]] requires
cumulative budget accounting across delegation hops and states that its
composition result assumes serialized admission. Its limitations identify
attacks spanning multiple sessions as residual risk requiring durable lineage
state. A [[https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15906][September 2026 survey of agent
authorization]] independently identifies runtime enforcement and
aggregation bounds as unresolved architecture gaps. The survey is a map of
the literature; this example does not treat it as experimental evidence.

[[https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2026.1800407/full][LATTICE
(2026)]] evaluates an immutable action bundle against in-process mutation
and reports successful checks for its tested vectors. That addresses a
different time-of-check/time-of-use surface from two workers concurrently
reading a shared budget. This case models the latter boundary. It is our
proposed Cedar/POO projection, not a reproduction of any paper's runtime.

| Source observation | Proposed Cedar/POO projection | Unverified boundary |
|--------------------+-------------------------------+---------------------|
| A delegated budget cannot be reset at each worker. | =Local= and =Shared= are independent policy owners; =Integrated= C4-composes both. | A trusted host must bind both workers to the same delegation and shared ledger. |
| Per-worker compliance can still exceed a parent-wide cap. | The two-worker trace shows =Local= allowing both sends, while =Integrated= allows only the first. | The example does not discover all descendant workers or enforce a distributed quota itself. |
| Two concurrent checks can both see an unspent shared budget. | Lean shows both stale checks Allow, whereas serialized admission rejects the second proposal. | Cedar evaluates requests; atomic reserve/commit and tool execution remain host duties. |
| [[https://docs.cedarpolicy.com/auth/authorization.html][Cedar skips policies that error during evaluation]]. | =Integrated= overlays the broad permit with both positive budget predicates, so a missing counter cannot leave a valid grant. | A host must still validate complete request context and treat diagnostics as an operational failure. |
| Incident response must stop both workers without rewriting permanent controls. | =Incident= extends one egress veto; =Recovered= removes only that veto. | An external authority determines when the incident is resolved. |

The workers, destination, and budget values in
[[file:SharedBudget.lean][SharedBudget.lean]] are illustrative. The model
checks a trusted =State= supplied to Cedar's request context. A production
host must authenticate worker lineage, validate counter ranges, serialize
decision plus reservation across every worker, bind approval to the exact
tool call, and keep the shared ledger durable. A Cedar Allow from an old
snapshot is not an execution permit after another worker has committed.

* Decision and race matrix

=A= means Allow and =D= means Deny. The default parent-wide cap is one
external send; each worker also has a local cap of one. The separate
same-worker row uses a shared cap of three to isolate the local control.

| POO root | Worker A then B, shared cap 1 | Worker A twice, shared cap 3 | First otherwise valid send during incident |
|----------+-------------------------------+------------------------------+--------------------------------------------|
| =Base= | A, A | A, A | A |
| =Local= | A, A | A, D | A |
| =Shared= | A, D | A, A | A |
| =Integrated= | A, D | A, D | A |
| =Incident= | D, D | D, D | D |
| =Recovered= | A, D | A, D | A |

The =Local= branch leaves the fanout hole; the =Shared= branch leaves the
same-worker hole when the parent has a larger aggregate allowance.
=Integrated= closes both in the serialized model. An already spent shared
budget denies the next proposal without changing state. The named Lean
cases and pre-admission contexts are exported step by step to the official
Cedar Rust authorizer. A worker absent from the bound delegation is denied
before invoking Cedar authorization.

The two missing-field requests expose a Cedar-specific failure mode. With
=Base=, missing =localUsed= still permits a send. If the final root depended
only on budget =forbid= policies, an error in a veto could leave that permit
effective. =Integrated= overlays the permit with both positive budget checks:
missing =localUsed= or =sharedUsed= then yields no satisfied permit and Deny.
The Lean decisions and official Rust decisions agree for both malformed
requests. The intermediate =Local= and =Shared= roots illustrate partial
owners; only =Integrated= is the proposed final deployment root.

The two checks against =S0= each return Allow. The Lean
=staleDoubleCommitExceedsBudget= result deliberately applies both updates
without rechecking and reaches =sharedUsed= of =2=. Under =replay=, which
commits the first decision before checking the second, the decisions are
=[true, false]= and =sharedUsed= of =1=. This finite example exposes the race;
it does not implement a distributed transaction or prove its correctness.

* Composition and proof receipts

| Root | Owner-local edit | Materialized policy bodies | Fresh body work against stated baseline |
|------+------------------+----------------------------+----------------------------------------|
| =Base= | Extend delegated send grant | 1 | 1 |
| =Local= | Extend worker-local budget veto | 2 | 1 vs =Base= |

Six standalone Cedar revisions contain 15 materialized policy occurrences.
Lean declares five distinct policy bodies and six local edits, including
the final permit overlay and recovery removal. This is a source-structure count, not a performance
measurement. The incident revision adds one fresh body, recovery adds none,
and unchanged certificates are reused when the authorization certificate
is reclosed. Cedar's upstream soundness theorem applies to the resulting
snapshot. The [[file:SharedBudgetExport.lean][exporter]] sends every named
request to the official Rust Cedar authorizer and checks all generated
=.cedar= revisions after rendering and reparsing.

* Cross-agent sensitive-read and egress composition

[[https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15888v1][Bounded Agents]] treats prior actions and delegated budgets as accumulated
session state, while limiting its composition soundness claim to complete
restrictions and serialized admission. The [[https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15906][2026 authorization architecture
survey]] identifies runtime enforcement and aggregation bounds as open gaps.
The earlier session and fanout examples model those concerns separately.
[[file:CrossAgentEgress.lean][CrossAgentEgress.lean]] combines them in one proposed workflow: worker A
reads a sensitive document and worker B proposes an external send under the
same delegation. This is a new model of the boundary, not a reproduction of
either paper's experiments or a claim that all agent exfiltration is solved.

The model starts from the existing fanout budget policy model. It reuses
the bounded-session read, history, and intent policy bodies after widening
their principal scope to workers authenticated by the host. =Model.extend=
stages three independent owners; =Model.mix= compiles the C4-composed
=CrossAgentGoverned= root through LeanPOO. The resulting policy set has six
surviving owner contributions: a send permit, shared and local budgets, a
read permit, delegated intent, and sensitive-read history. =CrossAgentIncident=
adds one egress freeze; =CrossAgentRecovered= removes just that edit.

| Trace | Worker-local history | Delegation-shared history |
|-------+----------------------+---------------------------|
| A reads secret; B sends externally | Both steps Allow: B's local ledger never saw A's read. | Read Allows; send Denies. |
| A reads public document; B sends on-task | Both Allow. | Both Allow. |
| A sends, then B sends with one shared export slot | First Allows; second Denies. | First Allows; second Denies. |

The same Cedar policies give different answers when the host supplies
worker-local versus delegation-shared =sensitiveSeen=. This is the concrete
aggregation gap: adding a history policy alone does not bind state across
workers. The Lean =admit= transition serializes the finite policy replay
example, and a denied send leaves the shared budget unchanged. =admit= is
diagnostic and does not consume an approval instance; Host admission uses
=prepare/commit=. A production host still
must authenticate lineage, derive the shared history from all relevant
reads, bind the checked output to the actual tool effect, and serialize
admission with durable state. Neither Cedar nor LeanPOO supplies that host
transaction automatically.

=prepare= and =commit= specify the host-facing boundary more precisely. A
ticket binds the C4 root, its exact compiled Cedar policies, ledger view,
state epoch, stable approval instance ID, proposed call, and payload digest.
=prepare= checks the approval's exact effect and remaining uses.
=commit= rechecks those fields and current Cedar authorization, consumes one
use from the [[file:../../../../CedarPooSpec/Admission/AuthorityConsumption.lean][public authority ledger]], and advances the epoch on success.
Lean checks three failure
paths: two sends prepared on one budget snapshot cannot both commit; changing
the payload after approval fails; and a sensitive read invalidates a
previously prepared send before a refreshed send is denied by shared
history. The digest and ticket in this model are values supplied by the
host, not cryptographic credentials. A deployment must derive the digest
from the exact tool-call bytes, authenticate the approval instance and ticket,
persist the ledger, and make compare-and-swap plus effect reservation atomic.

=consumedApprovalCannotBeReissued= demonstrates a separate limit from the
Cedar aggregate budget: after one send, Cedar still allows another send, but
the same one-use approval instance cannot issue a fresh ticket. A distinct
approval instance can execute while budget remains
(=independentApprovalCanStillExecute=). Old tickets, effect substitution, and
duplicate approval IDs fail closed. =prepare= does not reserve a use: competing
tickets may be prepared, and the Host must serialize their commits.

The [[file:../../../../CedarPooSpec/Admission/PolicySnapshot.lean][PolicySnapshot API]] closes a distinct revision gap. A second native POO
construction publishes the same =CrossAgentGoverned= root name with an
additional veto on worker B. Worker A's selected send still receives an
error-free Allow, but =reloadedPolicyRejectsPreparedTicket= rejects its old ticket
solely because the compiled policy set changed. A fresh ticket under the
new model commits. =reloadedRootValid= checks the new root against the Cedar
schema. The public snapshot check distinguishes a compilation error from a
changed policy set; =reloadedPolicyDiagnosesRevision= checks the latter. The
exact policy list is a local structural revision witness, not a
cryptographic digest or authenticated deployment version; a Host must bind
the actual loaded model and persist its version across restarts.

* Why POO is used here

Native =extend= and =mix= construct the independently owned history, intent,
budget, and review controls. The policy snapshot is captured from the C4
compilation of that object. A change to the compiled Cedar policy set
therefore changes the ticket's authority even if the public root name stays
the same. A provenance-only edit with an identical compiled policy set is
not detected by this value; a deployment requiring that distinction must
also bind its publication or approval revision.
The ledger epoch, approval consumption, budget consumption, and idempotent
external effect are not POO edits: they are mutable execution state that the
Host must serialize. This pure model does not persist a grant across restarts
or prove exactly-once delivery to an external sink.

* New research comparison

[[https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10487][Temporary Authority, Permanent Effects]] studies durable commits after
their earlier authority witness becomes stale. [[https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01710][Beyond Single-Use Tokens]]
identifies semantic replay through fresh token issuance after retries or
replanning; token-local single use is insufficient without durable,
monotonic authorization state. The [[https://github.com/cedar-policy/cedar-spec/blob/main/cedar-lean/README.md][Cedar Lean README]] describes symbolic
equivalence and implication of policy sets, which answer policy behavior
questions but do not consume a Host ticket. The September 2026
[[https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-saha-aadp-04][AADP Internet-Draft]] describes a two-phase decision and enforcement contract
with reservations and idempotency; it remains work in progress, not an
implemented standard here. This example closes policy-revision binding in
the pure model. Durable semantic replay prevention and idempotent external
effects remain open Host integration work.

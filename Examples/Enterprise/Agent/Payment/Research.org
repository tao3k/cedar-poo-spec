#+TITLE: Payment Operations: Authorization, Audit, and Effect
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil

* Scope and executable model

As of 2026-10-01, this is a research map and synthetic assurance case, not a
deployed payment service. The public
[[file:../../../../CedarPooSpec/Vertical/FinancialServices/PaymentOperation.lean][PaymentOperation]] is a concrete instruction: direct or delegated authority
mode, payer account, origin, payment instrument or rail, beneficiary, amount,
asset, network, fee cap, mandate reference, nonce, policy epoch, and expiry.
A delegated instruction requires a mandate reference; its authenticity and
scope remain Host obligations. Its methods keep three decisions
separate:

| Method boundary | Owner | Current executable claim |
|-----------------+-------+--------------------------|
| Authorization | Cedar policy and Host binding | An error-free Allow for this operation and the exact audit-policy snapshot is required. |
| Audit | =PaymentAuditPolicy.accepts= | The policy selects any positive number of evidence stages. Every supplied item must be verified, current, affirmative, and bound to the operation and policy snapshot. |
| Effect reservation | =PaymentOperation.prepare= plus Host | Only an authorized, audited, fresh operation consumes its nonce and creates a pending attempt; an identical second reservation fails. Actual transfer execution remains Host-owned. |

AI model, human reviewer, deterministic rule, and external investigator are
*kinds of audit evidence*, not Payment subclasses or hardcoded steps. A
requirement selects source and minimum distinct actors. A policy may require
human actors to differ across stages and exclude the proposer. A negative or
escalated verdict blocks the audited operation; model output never creates a
Cedar Allow. The downstream [[file:PaymentAudit.lean][example]] chooses three stages
(one model, Risk human, Compliance human) and binds Cedar's
=PaymentGoverned= decision. The [[file:../../../../Tests/Enterprise/Agent/Payment/PaymentAudit.lean][regressions]] exercise one-, two-, and
three-stage policies, changed beneficiary or fee, adverse/unverified
evidence, same reviewer, stale epoch, expiry, replay, and amount denial. Run
=just check-payment-audit=.

This object-level split follows the [[https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/waller20260928a.htm][Federal Reserve's distinction]] between
agent-assisted and agent-delegated payments, and the [[https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md][AP2 specification's]]
separation of checkout, payment mandate, credential verification, and
processor effect. Our =instrument= and =mandateRef= are opaque identifiers, not
AP2-compliant credentials. The [[https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00060][protocol analysis preprint]] similarly motivates
checking bindings across lifecycle stages; it does not certify this model.

The =verified= flags are Host assertions. This code does not verify signatures,
run a risk model, persist a nonce, or execute a payment. A production Host
must authenticate each evidence source, canonicalize and sign the complete
operation including instrument-specific details, obtain the actual policy epoch
and error-free Cedar response, and persist the nonce with its pending attempt
before dispatch. Detection quality and reviewer load need measurement.
This pure contract follows the ownership boundary in
[[file:../../../../docs/rfc/0008-composable-control-objects.org][RFC 0008]]: Cedar owns authorization, the public library states control
requirements, and the Host owns authentication and effects.

* Payment attempt lifecycle and uncertain responses

The public [[file:../../../../CedarPooSpec/Vertical/FinancialServices/PaymentLifecycle.lean][PaymentLifecycle]] separates =reserved=, =submitted=,
=accepted=, =settled=, and =rejected=. =prepare= pairs the nonce reservation
with a pending attempt. =submit= permits one transition from =reserved= to
=submitted= and returns one =ProcessorRequest= carrying the complete payment
and its nonce as a stable idempotency key. The Host must atomically compare and
persist that transition before calling the processor, so a stale copy of
=reserved= cannot emit another command. An unanswered request stays =submitted=.
=statusQuery= permits a read-only lookup by the same key; after acceptance it
also carries the provider reference. Recovery records authenticated
=ProcessorEvidence= that matches the payment and key. It must not infer failure
from a timeout and resend the payment.

Processor acceptance is an acknowledgement, not settlement. A later
settlement or rejection must match the accepted provider reference. Direct
final outcomes from =submitted= are also modeled. Settlement and rejection
are terminal. The [[file:../../../../Tests/Enterprise/Agent/Payment/PaymentAudit.lean][regressions]] check no second submit,
acceptance without settlement, matching finality, forged or mismatched
observations, wrong idempotency keys, and terminal states.
=just check-payment-audit= compiles them.

The pure model does not supply a durable transaction, a processor idempotency
guarantee, an authenticated status query, or evidence of economic finality.
The Host must enforce these at the adapter boundary and reconcile an uncertain
response before declaring a final outcome. The local no-second-submit theorem
concerns this state machine; it does not prove that a remote processor applies
an effect at most once. A crash after persisting =submitted= but before sending
may leave an unsent pending request. If status lookup reports no record, this
model does not authorize a resend; the Host must hold it for reconciliation or
use a separately specified provider guarantee for safe same-key retry.

The [[https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00060][formal protocol study]] describes MPP-14 as a source-aligned symbolic trace:
spend is recorded, a response is lost, and retry accounts the same logical
request twice. Its patched reference atomically records a request key, cost,
and result state. This model addresses the *Host command* side of that pattern;
it does not reproduce the studied protocol or assert a deployed vulnerability.
The [[https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md][AP2 specification]] separately binds mandates and receipts to a checkout;
our nonce key is a local Host convention, not an AP2 credential or AP2-mandated
idempotency rule.

* Cross-stage audit-policy binding

The authorization receipt and every audit item now carry the *exact*
=PaymentAuditPolicy=, while =PaymentState= supplies the currently active
policy. =PaymentOperation.admissible= compares the authorization snapshot to
that active policy, and =PaymentAuditPolicy.accepts= checks every evidence
item against it. A three-stage authorization cannot be paired with a later
one-stage plan even when the Cedar policy epoch is unchanged. Recapturing an
authorization for the one-stage plan still cannot reuse evidence issued for
the three-stage plan. These are executable negative cases in
[[file:../../../../Tests/Enterprise/Agent/Payment/PaymentAudit.lean][PaymentAudit.lean]]. The public =changedAuditPolicyCannotReserve= theorem
proves the first rejection for any payment and evidence.

This is a matched, local response to the cross-stage binding questions in
[[https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00060][A Formal Analysis of Agent Payment Protocols]] and the state-binding
family in [[https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23858][Beyond the Mandate]] (both 2026 preprints). Their broader
protocol findings do not imply that any named provider has this exact bug.
The comparison is structural Lean equality, not a cryptographic digest or a
live policy-store read. The Host must authenticate evidence, read its actual
active audit policy, and compare/reserve it atomically with execution.

* Incident evidence and limits

The [[https://www.bitget.com/campaigns/bitget-security-incident-2026][Bitget disclosure]] reports that its security system detected abnormal
transfers after a third-party product compromise led to stolen internal
credentials and forged withdrawal commands that bypassed risk verification.
Its [[https://www.bitget.com/support/articles/12560603896305/][investigation update]] also attributes the compromise to a third-party
product. The public record does not establish whether AI detection was
deployed or whether it would have prevented the incident. We use the bypass
pattern to test whether execution independently verifies identity, exact
command binding, risk evidence, and release authority.

* Next research slices

1. Define canonical intent serialization and signed evidence verification;
   check substitution and replay against the public processor adapter.
2. Connect the approved policy epoch and whole-store authorization-delta
   review to an atomic Host publication/execution transaction.
3. Compare AP2 mandate semantics and delegated authorization with the
   synthetic model, then add protocol-level adversarial traces.
4. Evaluate a real detector on a documented dataset with false-negative,
   false-positive, latency, drift, and reviewer-load measurements. Do not
   present fixture assertions as detection performance.

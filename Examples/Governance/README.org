* The POO question

[[file:AttestedDataAccess.lean][AttestedDataAccess.lean]] asks what happens to a
composed authorization proof when the platform owner strengthens attestation.
The interesting result is the composition delta: which policy body changed,
which composition roots are affected, and which proof obligations can be
reused. Cedar already supplies authorization evaluation and validation; this
example uses those operations as evidence for a POO evolution claim.

The model combines a data subscription, owner approval, enclave measurement,
and organizational region rule. It is a composite reference scenario, not a
claim that these AWS products expose one Cedar policy interface. All people,
projects, datasets, and measurements in the Lean file are fictitious.

* Composition and evolution

=Base= has a project subscription permit and an inherited legacy broad permit.
=DataOwner=, =Platform=, and =Compliance= extend separate vetoes. =Governed=
composes those owners, overlays the project permit with an analytics-purpose
condition, and removes the legacy permit. =PlatformV2= overlays the attestation
veto to require freshness; =GovernedV2= composes the updated platform branch.

=compositionTraceConformsFully= checks the C4 order, eight applied edits, the
final removal, and the provenance of the platform policy: introduced by
=Platform=, last edited by =PlatformV2=. =broadPermitRiskDetected= shows why
removal is substantive: the owner branch alone can allow another project's
dataset through the legacy permit, while the integrated module denies it.

=impactIsLocalFully= computes affected roots from the module graph. The
platform revision affects =PlatformV2= and =GovernedV2=; =Governed=,
=DataOwner=, =Compliance=, and =Sandbox= remain outside that dependency cone.
=proofReuseFootprintExact= shows that the policy patch touches only the policy
validation obligation. =patchedCertificate= reuses the baseline certificate
and closes that one pending obligation; =certificate= transports the result to
the updated authorization snapshot and then applies Cedar's soundness theorem.
This is a checked proof reuse construction, not a timing or LOC benchmark.

The five =unchangedCases= keep the same decision across the revision.
=staleAttestationDeltaExact= isolates the intended difference: a stale
attestation was allowed by the baseline policy and is denied after the
platform overlay. The finite cases help inspect the evolution but are not a
claim of complete real-world authorization coverage.

The application must verify attestation and freshness, authenticate the
approver, establish subscription state, bind all projected facts to this
request, and enforce the returned decision. Those actions are external to the
proof object and to Cedar evaluation.

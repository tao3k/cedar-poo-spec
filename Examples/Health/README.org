* Clinical break-glass and patient preferences

[[file:ClinicalBreakGlass.lean][ClinicalBreakGlass.lean]] is an executable,
source-grounded model of patient-record access. It combines documented
features from two deployed EHR settings; it is not a transcription of either
hospital's entire production policy. The principal, patient, and facility
identifiers are fictive. The example does not contain patient data.

The split into Privacy, Security, and Emergency policy owners is a design
assumption for exploring composition. It is not reported as the organization
of any cited deployment.

** Why POO is needed

The clinical base, privacy rule, device rule, and emergency rule have separate
owners. A C4 diamond composes those branches once. The integrated module
overlays the emergency rule with patient, session, treatment-purpose, and
device conditions, then removes a legacy broad permit. The executable
=preIntegrationRiskDetected= theorem shows why the removal matters: before
integration, the inherited broad permit allows a cross-site read without a
break-glass grant; after integration it is denied.

=compileWithTrace= records all seven applied edits, including removal of the
legacy permit; its surviving-policy projection reports who introduced and
last edited each policy. These APIs close a diagnostics gap exposed by the
case: a materialized Cedar policy set alone did not explain why a permit was
absent or how an emergency policy reached the final set.
The patient-preference change alters entity data
without editing any policy module. =preferenceRevisionFootprintExact= proves
that the entity-validation obligation is selected while policy validation,
schema validation, and request validation are unaffected.

Across Base, Privacy, Security, Emergency, and Integrated, separately stored
snapshots would repeat 15 policy records (2 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 4). The module
source defines six policy bodies and seven explicit edits. This counts
structural duplication in the example; it does not measure production LOC,
review time, or maintenance cost.

** Proof receipts and limits

=scenarioConformsFully= checks all twelve named requests against the
compiled policy set, Cedar validation, Cedar authorization, and empty runtime
policy-error lists. =caseNamesUnique= prevents ambiguous case references.
=provenanceConformsFully= checks C4 order and policy origin;
=removalRecordedFully= checks the final removal event. A closed
authorization certificate transports Cedar's upstream soundness theorem for
the emergency request. These are machine-checked claims about this Lean
model and its finite scenario table. They do not establish that a real
hospital's policy, data projection, session service, or audit pipeline is
correct.

The application must authenticate the clinician, establish whether emergency
access is justified, bind the grant to a patient and session, verify device
posture, enforce Cedar's response, and write a durable audit event before
releasing data. Neither Cedar's Allow nor the proof certificate proves that
those external actions occurred. A later domain integration must supply and
verify those effects; the generic =cedar-poo-spec= adapter does not impersonate
an EHR or an AWS Verified Permissions service.

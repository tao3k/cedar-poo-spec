#+OPTIONS: toc:nil

* Evidence and bounded problem

[[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42217263/][Clinical agents fail silently on patient identity]] (2026) tested agents
copying medical codes into EHR records after patient-identity faults were
introduced. Its authors report that agents often wrote through a swapped
patient header or subtle identifier change. This example projects that
failure mode onto a proposed provider-to-payer authorization workflow; the
paper did not test this exact workflow.

[[https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29366][AI-Generated Prior Authorization Letters]] (2026) evaluated 45
physician-validated synthetic cases. It found clinically strong letters but
recurrent administrative gaps, including billing codes, requested duration,
and follow-up plans. Here those three fields are separate host-validated
facts at the submission boundary. Cedar does not inspect the letter text or
establish its clinical accuracy.

[[https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/publicsector/architecting-hipaa-compliant-ai-agents-to-safeguard-health-data-with-aws/][AWS's 2026 healthcare agent architecture]] traces a lumbar-MRI prior
authorization through retrieval, model output, memory, submission, and logs,
showing where PHI can spread. [[https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/cms-interoperability-prior-authorization-final-rule-cms-0057-f][CMS-0057-F]] calls for impacted payers'
Prior Authorization APIs to expose documentation requirements and support
requests and responses. This example models only a finite authorization
decision at the provider's reads and outbound submission. It does not claim
HIPAA or FHIR conformance, a payer decision, or a live EHR integration.

* Connected workflow and POO ownership

[[file:PriorAuthorization.lean][PriorAuthorization.lean]] uses synthetic patient,
record, agent, and payer identifiers. The provider agent reads a clinical
record and a billing record for the same patient, prepares an AI-assisted
draft, and proposes a submission to the payer. Five owners extend the same
=Base= root through =Model.extend= and compose through =Model.mix=:

| Owner | Local control | Gap if used alone |
|-------+---------------+-------------------|
| =Identity= | Deny wrong-patient reads and submission without clinical evidence. | It does not validate billing requirements. |
| =Administration= | Require billing evidence, code, requested duration, and follow-up. | It does not bind the patient or limit disclosure. |
| =Privacy= | Deny a draft marked as containing a raw record. | It does not validate administrative fields. |
| =PayerRules= | Deny submission against stale payer requirements. | It does not govern record access. |
| =Temporal= | Deny reads or submission after delegation expires. | It does not validate the draft. |

=Governed= combines these owners. =Incident= adds one submission freeze;
=Recovered= removes only that freeze and retains every other owner. The
=composedOwnersPresent= Lean result checks that all five owner contributions
survive compilation. =Governed= also overlays the three inherited read and
submit permits with positive predicates. This closes the missing-context
case where a Cedar =forbid= could error and be skipped while a broad permit
still grants. =missingDisclosureFactCannotGrant= checks a request with the
disclosure fact omitted. The official Rust authorizer separately replays this
malformed request and confirms Deny despite the expected policy errors. This
is a proposed POO decomposition, not the reported
organization of any cited hospital or payer.

* Executable contrast

| Workflow change | =Base= or partial root | =Governed= |
|-----------------+--------------------------+------------|
| Clinical record belongs to another patient. | =Base= allows the read. | Identity owner denies the read. |
| Clinical and billing reads complete, but a billing code is absent. | =Identity= allows submission. | Administration owner denies submission. |
| Code, duration, and follow-up are present, but raw clinical record is in the draft. | The other owners do not reject this fact. | Privacy owner denies submission. |
| Payer requirements became stale or delegation expired after evidence retrieval. | Earlier reads remain recorded. | The later submission is denied. |
| Incident freezes a valid submission, then recovery removes the freeze. | Other owner policies remain. | Recovered submission allows; raw-record disclosure still denies. |

=casesExact= checks the finite Lean decisions. The
[[file:ValidatedExport.lean][schema-bound exporter]] sends all 45
pre-admission requests to the official Cedar Rust validator and authorizer;
the checked generated Cedar files are under [[file:Policies/][Policies/]].
These checks establish agreement for the modeled facts and requests, not
clinical safety, model-output correctness, or production compliance.

The records are synthetic identifiers and projected facts. A downstream
application must build the real EHR and billing connectors, verify patient
identity independently of the agent, validate the letter fields and payer
requirements, classify or minimize disclosure, authenticate delegation,
persist the admitted history, and bind the checked draft to the actual
submission. [[https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11510][AgentLeak]] reports internal-channel leakage through inter-agent
messages and shared memory; those channels are an additional research and
integration boundary outside this finite submission example.
